# Sera Moss

Sera is an estate surveyor employed by a village council, not the mansion's owners. She came to settle a boundary dispute that affects several homes downstream. The official survey she was given omits a service path; she has begun drawing the property anew from observation. Her map is unfinished, and she knows that a route on paper is no proof that anyone used it tonight.

**Perspective.** Sera thinks in entrances, distances, wet footprints, and changes to surfaces. She has little patience for grand motives until the physical route is possible. A closed door matters because it changes who could pass, not because it makes a satisfying story. She is alert to how the house was built to make some workers invisible.

**Private stakes.** If the disputed land goes to the estate, families she knows could lose access to the stream. A council clerk warned her to keep quiet until her measurements are complete. She wants an honest boundary record and worries that her presence at night will be mistaken for trespass or worse.

**Voice and behavior.** She talks in concrete directions and checks a speaker's description against the place. Her humor is dry and brief. She tends to walk instead of sit, favors the art room and central corridor routes, and pauses at thresholds. She will admit when she has not seen a room herself. She distrusts authority only when it asks her to ignore evidence.

**In conversation.** Sera asks which way a witness entered or left and whether they actually saw the route. She can be persuaded by a careful account that corrects her map. If she is the killer, she may exploit real knowledge of routes, but cannot disclose impossible movement or unseen events. Her map and biography are motives and perspective; only round facts establish guilt.
## Human appearance

Sera has Black features, a deep brown complexion, strong cheeks and long dark natural curls. Her moss field coat, map, compass and shaped field boots suit her practical work.
